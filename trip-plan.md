## 1. Acadia National Park

**Designation:** National Park
**State:** Maine (ME)

Acadia protects the highest rocky headlands along the U.S. Atlantic coastline, with 158 miles of hiking trails, 45 miles of carriage roads, and 27 miles of historic motor roads. It draws about 4 million visits a year — one of the ten most-visited national parks. Fall temperatures run 30–70°F (-1 to 21°C), with the first frost typically in mid-October, so pack layers and expect cool mornings. The park is open year-round.

**Getting in:** $35 private vehicle (7 days), $30 motorcycle, $20 per person on foot/bike; timed-entry vehicle reservations are $6 where required. Note the park also charges a $100 nonresident fee for non-U.S. visitors 16+ without an annual/America the Beautiful pass.

### Current alerts (3 active)

1. **Eastern Section of Beech Mountain Loop Trail Closed for Repairs** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 9/30/2026)*
   The eastern section is closed from a junction just beyond the parking lot to just below the summit at the fire tower (closure began Sept 21). The summit is still reachable via three alternates: the outer loop of the Beech Mountain Loop Trail, Beech South Ridge Trail, and Beech West Ridge Trail. **For us:** plan the alternate routes if Beech Mountain is on the list — don't count on the eastern approach.

2. **Schoodic Mountain Road Closed for Repairs** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 9/30/2026)*
   Closed from Schoodic Loop Road to the summit of Schoodic Head, with heavy equipment operating for resurfacing, regrading, and ditching (since Aug 31). **For us:** no vehicle or trailhead access at Schoodic Head; skip this area or view it from elsewhere on the Schoodic Peninsula.

3. **Southern Section of Ocean Path Closed for Repairs** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 9/16/2026)*
   Closed from the trailhead across from the Otter Point parking lot to the trail's southern terminus (since Aug 31). **For us:** the Ocean Path signature stretch south of Otter Point is off-limits; plan coastal walking around it rather than through.

**Bottom line:** all three are repair-driven closures of specific trails/roads — no safety hazards, wildlife issues, or facility-wide problems reported. The rest of the park, including the carriage roads and most trail mileage, is unaffected.

## 2. Shenandoah National Park

**Designation:** National Park
**State:** Virginia (VA)

About 75 miles from Washington, D.C., Shenandoah covers 200,000+ acres of waterfalls, vistas, and wooded hollows, stretching 105 miles along the Blue Ridge with four entrances (Front Royal, Thornton Gap, Swift Run Gap, Rockfish Gap). Skyline Drive is the only public road through the park and is periodically closed in sections during inclement weather — though the park remains enterable on foot. The mountain runs usually 10°F cooler than the valley, so dress in layers and check the forecast.

**Getting in:** $30 private vehicle (7 days), $25 motorcycle, $15 per person. Important for our group: the park is fully cashless at entrance stations (since July 1, 2025) — credit, debit, or mobile payments only. Sign up for text alerts by sending SHENALERTS to 888777 for real-time Skyline Drive status.

### Current alerts (7 active)

1. **Active Closure** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 10/14/2026)*
   Due to recent wildlife interactions, the Naked Creek backcountry zone is closed to **all recreation** until further notice. A camping closure also remains in effect on the Appalachian Trail from Lewis Mountain to Big Meadows, including the Bearfence AT Hut; the AT there is still open for day-use only. **For us:** hard boundary — no entry to Naked Creek, and no overnight camping on that AT stretch. If we're day-hiking the AT between Lewis Mountain and Big Meadows, that's still fine; we just can't camp or use the Bearfence Hut.

2. **Emergency Phone Line Unavailable** *(Caution — potential hazard, last updated 10/10/2026)*
   The park's emergency number, 1-800-732-0911, is currently out of service. **For us:** in an emergency, dial 911 — everyone should have this in their phones before we head out, since the usual park emergency line won't work.

3. **Infrastructure Improvements to Old Rag Trailhead** *(Information, last updated 4/22/2026)*
   The Old Rag Trailhead main parking lot is closed to all vehicle and pedestrian traffic while improvements run through November 2026. Access is via the existing overflow lots. Day-use tickets are still required March 1–November 30, in addition to the entrance fee. **For us:** use the overflow lots, and sort out tickets in advance if Old Rag is on the itinerary.

4. **Ticket Required to Hike Old Rag** *(Information, last updated 1/17/2024)*
   March 1 through November 30, anyone hiking Old Rag needs a day-use ticket obtained **before arriving**. **For us:** this is squarely in our fall window — book tickets ahead of time or pick a different summit.

5. **Shenandoah Moved to Fully Cashless Fee Collection** *(Information, last updated 4/22/2026)*
   Entrance stations accept only credit cards, debit cards, or mobile payments. **For us:** nobody should show up with only cash for the entrance fee.

6. **Parking at Boundary Access Areas** *(Caution — potential hazard, last updated 11/20/2025)*
   When using boundary trailheads, park only in designated areas; unauthorized parking may mean towing at the owner's expense. Park on the right side of the road approaching Thompson Hollow, up to the "No Parking" sign before the private driveway. **For us:** carpool discipline matters at boundary access points — towed cars would end the trip early.

7. **Get Skyline Drive Status Updates** *(Information, last updated 9/29/2025)*
   Text SHENALERTS to 888777 for closure/reopening and safety notifications. **For us:** worth having the whole group subscribed, since Skyline Drive sections can close with weather at short notice.

**Bottom line:** the big operational items are the Naked Creek/AT camping closure and the dead emergency line (use 911), plus Old Rag logistics — overflow parking plus pre-purchased day-use tickets. Everything else is logistical (cashless, parking, alerts signup).

## 3. Great Smoky Mountains National Park

**Designation:** National Park
**States:** North Carolina (NC) and Tennessee (TN)

Ridge upon ridge of forest straddling the NC/TN border — America's most visited national park, renowned for plant and animal diversity, ancient mountains, and Southern Appalachian culture. Elevations range from roughly 875 ft to 6,643 ft (Kuwohi area), and temperatures can swing 10–20°F from base to top; clear skies low down don't guarantee the same higher up. Primary roads (Newfound Gap Road/US-441, Little River Road, Laurel Creek Road) are open 24/7 year-round, weather permitting; secondary roads close seasonally. Main entrances: Gatlinburg, TN; Townsend, TN; and Cherokee, NC.

**Getting in:** there is no entrance fee. Instead, **every vehicle parking longer than 15 minutes needs a parking tag** — Daily $5, Weekly $15, or Annual $40, available for all vehicle sizes/types.

### Current alerts (4 active)

1. **Cherokee Orchard Road closed to pedestrian traffic** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 10/20/2026)*
   Closed to pedestrians due to bear activity; all visitors must stay in their vehicles and must not stop or park in the roadway. **For us:** drive-through only on Cherokee Orchard Road — no roadside stops, no walking out of the car. Worth pairing with the bear-awareness programming below, and treat it as a reminder of how active bears are right now in the park.

2. **Park Headquarters Road is closed** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 6/26/2026)*
   Closed for rehabilitation work on the Sugarlands water and wastewater systems. **For us:** plan access to the Sugarlands area around this closure — check current routing to Sugarlands Visitor Center before we go.

3. **Straight Fork and the one-way section of Balsam Mountain Road closed** *(Park Closure — area inaccessible, last updated 11/12/2025)*
   Straight Fork and the one-way section of Balsam Mountain Road (Heintooga Round-Bottom Road) are closed. **For us:** scratch the Balsam Mountain/Heintooga loop and Straight Fork from the route plan.

4. **Most visitors need a parking tag** *(Information, last updated 2/8/2024)*
   All vehicles parking for longer than 15 minutes require a tag: Daily $5, Weekly $15, Annual $40. **For us:** if we're in the park for multiple days, the weekly tag is the obvious buy — make sure every vehicle in the convoy has one.

**Bottom line:** the main live safety item is the bear-activity closure on Cherokee Orchard Road (stay in vehicles), plus two road closures to route around and the parking-tag requirement. No trail or backcountry closures reported park-wide.
